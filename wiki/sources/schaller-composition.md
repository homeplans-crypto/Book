## Key claims (each citable)

- **Composition is not formulaic** — "a bit of a non-question," like camera settings: you use different composition for what you need. **Distrust anyone stating composition rules as definite** — "it doesn't exist."
- **Rule of thirds** — 2/3 open, 1/3 a concentrated viewpoint; tied to the golden ratio / natural spiral; decide what matters most and place it on the third (Sloane Square mannequins, lockdown; low flat angle).
- **Trimming the fat** — *the* most important rule, applies to any style: "leave the essentials and **not one pixel more**." Excess info that doesn't aid the story is damage. Be **decisive at the time of shooting**; composition is linked to a conceptual idea + the confidence to execute it. (Vietnam: three boys watching football — leading lines from net/treeline, nothing clashing tonally or physically, subjects offset and balanced by the action they watch.)
- **Get it right in person, not by cropping** — "nothing beats being in the right place with the camera"; use the whole sensor/film; crop only to fine-tune.
- **Leading lines** funnel the eye to the subject; **light & tone are the sidekick** — in B&W the subject must sit in good light or the lines distract. 16mm angled up to exaggerate line drama; dark tree tones placed deliberately to balance.
- **Center of frame** = power (battlefield/chessboard/boxing-ring; Louvre's central figures) — but still needs harmony with everything else.
- **Grid lines** — uses the camera's **3×3 grid** a lot, *especially* with ultra-wide (16mm) where level is hard to judge and edges distort. (On M11.)
- **Framing & subframing** — find the "box within the frame" (a light, an arch, a structure) and position so lines run into the frame edge into that subframe; plan the spot, then **adapt** to what actually walks in ("hope for the best, be quick to adapt").
- **Balance / unorthodox composition** — non-traditional frames work when elements connect (Isle of Wight: distant running kid + woman, different scale, joined by a sand leading line). "**Gear doesn't matter** — it's ideas, intent, execution; composition is a vessel for the idea."
- **Use the environment** — street photographers have "full license to use the world"; **symmetry / repetition / geometry** (the NYC Oculus; glass for a split aspect). Not unique to street.
- **Named composition influences**: Ansel Adams, Bill Brandt, Steve McCurry, **Fan Ho** (Hong Kong, 1950s — "one of my biggest inspirations"), Cecil Beaton (portraits), Sebastião Salgado.
- **Think for yourself; shoot with intention** — "what am I trying to do here?" Random walk-and-shoot "is not photography"; "photography is making order out of your environment with a camera."
- He again notes **boosting the subject "a tiny bit in post"** (self-portrait) — the in-camera frame is the base, post finishes it.
