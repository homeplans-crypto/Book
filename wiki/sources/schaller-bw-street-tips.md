## Key claims (each citable)

1. **Shoot B&W on purpose** — go out with B&W *only* in mind; a color mindset confuses you (color is a different skill set). ~**1 month of daily B&W** → marked improvement.
2. **Focus on what makes B&W interesting** — not color: **texture, tonality, contrast, light**. "A lady in a red coat looks great in color; in B&W it's just one shade of gray." Seek tonal contrast and light that varies across the frame.
3. **Be realistic about the light you have** — don't force your will on the environment; let it dictate. Rain → reflections + the city/countryside *mood*; sun → backlighting + shadows. **"There's no such thing as bad light"** — accept it and draw with it.
4. **Make good use of your environment** — geometry, context, reflections; not just the human element. The **"magic trio": good subject + good environment + good light** = "you're winning."
5. **Contrast & tonality — [[entities/ansel-adams|Ansel Adams]] Zone System** — every tone represented black→white + greys between. You don't need extreme negative space / contrast, but a full tonal range is the baseline.
6. **Perspective** — don't always shoot at head height (that's the world as we see it daily); high/low/odd angles show it differently.
7. **Editing** — the captured material defines the edit; if it isn't in the frame you can't force it later. **"Editing is varnishing a table that's already well made"** — the finish, not the process.
