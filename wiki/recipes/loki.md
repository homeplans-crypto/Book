A high-contrast, "inky" [[entities/classic-negative]] look by [[entities/mehdi-berrada]]: Highlight +4 / Shadow +4 maxes the tone curve for crushed, glossy blacks and a hard light/shadow split; Color 0 and Color Chrome off keep it desaturated and graphic; WB R+3 B−5 warms it; Clarity +3 and Sharpness +3 add grit. The author recommends underexposing −2/3 EV and shooting it in bright, contrasty light. Built for harsh sun and strong directional light — the opposite end of the spectrum from the soft recipes; not for flat overcast (the look collapses without hard shadows). Caveat: Adobe RGB color space, JPEG; DR100 (no highlight protection — exposure discipline matters here).

> Source note (resolved 2026-05-17, [[synthesis/debriefs]]): **transfers to the [[entities/x100vi]] / X-Trans 5 — the look is intact** (crushed blacks, warm near-monochrome, glossy bloom). It is not broken on the newer sensor. The real risk is the recipe's own design, not the sensor: DR100 + Highlight +4 clips bright point sources hard (lamps blow out and halate); Shadow +4 crushes — intentional on textured/directional subjects, but kills detail in already-low-key scenes.

> Self note 2026-05-17 (shot, [[synthesis/debriefs]]): **Color 0 + warm WB makes this behave like a warm B&W, not a color recipe.** At blue hour it *erased* the blue palette and turned dusk into a warm, dark, dramatic scene — striking, but not a blue-hour look (use [[recipes/soft-color-universe]] for that). In raking/directional light on a textured subject (grass against dark mulch) it excelled — isolation, grit, drama. Confirms its domain is **contrast + texture + directional light**, broader than just "harsh sun." `best_for` widened.

Contrast counterpart to [[recipes/pastel-vibes]] — both Classic Negative, opposite intent: Loki maximizes contrast/grit, Pastel Vibes minimizes it.
