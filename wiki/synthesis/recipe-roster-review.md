Flagged here rather than silently rewritten.

## Rubric (score 1–5, must trace to a citable wiki line)

1. **Practice alignment** — serves the owner's real conditions/subjects?
2. **Earned evidence** — experience-confirmed, distinct strength?
3. **Distinctiveness** — unique niche vs. overlaps a sibling?
4. **Reliability** — portable, or condition-dependent with a precondition that *actually occurs* in practice?
5. **Aesthetic fit** — owner actually likes & reaches for it?

## Decisions log

### 2026-05-18 — Schaller recipes re-examined against the ingested Schaller videos
- Three Schaller video transcripts now in [[synthesis/schaller-technique]]. Re-tested both Schaller recipes against his own stated method.
- **Schaller Dark — no dial change (the videos confirm it).** EV −2/3 = his own "underexpose, recover from shadows" `[NS]`; Highlight +1 (harsher/brighter highlights) matches his deliberate pure-white blown highlights by the subject's head `[NS]`; the Shadow +4 crush is the *deliberate exception* to his [[entities/ansel-adams|Zone-System]] default `[BW]`, not a fault. Internally coherent; value added is in shooting-discipline notes, not dials.
- **Schaller Luminous — two changes, both reverting to the original spec, now author-justified (A/B-gated, NOT committed):** DR200 → **DR400** (full tonal range needs highlight headroom — the Zone-System default `[BW]` is Luminous's identity) and EV +2/3 → **~0 metered for highlights** (Schaller *protects* highlights / underexposes `[NS]`; +2/3 brightens toward the clipping he avoids, and at DR200 there is no headroom — DR200+EV+2/3 is the least Schaller-consistent pairing). The JPEG no-post constraint ("editing is varnish" `[BW]`; we can't RAW-boost shadows) *strengthens* the in-camera-latitude case.
- Validation #4 sharpened to a 3-way (Luminous DR400/EV~0 vs Luminous committed DR200/EV+2/3 vs Dark, same frame). Guardrail intact: proposal only — no camera change until shot and filed as a `debrief`.
- Shooting-discipline notes (cited) added to both recipe pages: blinkies place pure black/white by the subject; find the contrast that's there, don't expect the recipe to manufacture it; wet pavement = highlight bursts; subframe; shoot with intention.
