# Saturation as light-compensation (gray & rain)

Flat, gray, overcast, or wet light strips color out of a scene — everything trends toward muted and lifeless. The counter-move: shoot a **high-saturation recipe** so the look puts back the color the light isn't delivering. Saturation is matched to the *deficit*, not the abundance.

## Principle

- **Dull light → bold recipe.** A heavily-saturated Velvia-class recipe ([[recipes/bay-area-fortia]]) lifts gray scenes and rain into something with life; neutral recipes go drab in the same light.
- **Already-colorful light → restrained recipe.** The same bold recipe over-eggs scenes that already have strong color (warm artificial light, neon) — reds clip, separation is lost. Use a neutral/soft recipe there.
- **Rain is an asset with the right look.** Wet surfaces add reflections and saturation-friendly sheen; a vivid recipe turns "bad weather" into a reason to shoot, not a reason to stop.
- This reframes the long-standing flat-overcast problem: it isn't "no recipe works in gray," it's "the *bold* recipe works in gray."
