# Brian — Photographic Style Statement (corrected)

Provided 2026-07-03 during the reconvened X100VI recipe panel, with the note: "This is what the description should have been." Supersedes the Aesthetic section of `brian-photographic-preferences.md` where they differ.

---

My photographic style is quiet, architectural, and place-driven. I am drawn to streets, buildings, sidewalks, curbs, rails, trees, lamps, pavement, weather, and the small visual structures that make a place feel specific. I am less interested in dramatic subject-chasing or generic cinematic street photography, and more interested in photographing the environment itself as a landscape.

The goal is believable realism with emotional memory attached. The image should feel like being there, not like a filter was imposed on the scene. I prefer clean structure, readable depth, controlled highlights, meaningful shadows, and strong texture. Detail matters, especially in surfaces, materials, edges, streets, walls, foliage, and architectural elements.

Color should be restrained but alive. I do not want overcooked saturation, heavy teal-orange grading, pastel flatness, or generic film-recipe dominance. Reds and local earth/material colors should feel present without becoming loud. Warmth should usually be subtle. I often prefer realistic color that has been gently shaped rather than obviously stylized.

At night or blue hour, my style is "streets as landscapes." I want the street to remain legible as a place: pavement, building edges, lamps, railings, trees, sky, and local color should all hold together. Artificial lights should be controlled, not blown out. The image should have atmosphere, depth, and structure rather than becoming a generic night scene.

My influences lean toward quiet composition, layered light, strong shadows, human-scale architecture, and atmospheric realism. Think less "social media cinematic" and more composed, textured, observant, realistic, and slightly poetic. The best version of the style feels clean, grounded, and specific to place, with enough restraint that the scene remains believable.
